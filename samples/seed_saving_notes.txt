Seed saving notes (sample document)

These notes were written for the Kioo demonstration. They are an example of community knowledge that can be lost when the person who holds it is no longer there.

Choosing plants. Choose seed from the healthiest plants, not from the largest fruit alone. Mark the plants you want with a strip of cloth while they are still growing, so the seed is not eaten or sold by mistake.

Beans. Leave bean pods on the plant until they are dry and rattle when shaken. Pick them on a dry morning. Spread the pods in a shaded, airy place for one week, then shell them by hand.

Tomatoes. Scoop the seeds from a fully ripe tomato into a jar with a little water. Leave the jar for three days. A layer of mould will form on top. This is normal and helps remove the jelly around the seed. Rinse the seeds and dry them on a plate, not on paper, because they stick to paper.

Storing seed. Seeds must be completely dry before storage. Store them in clean glass jars or tins with tight lids, in a cool, dark place. Write the name of the crop and the year on every jar. Add a spoonful of dry ash or dry rice to the jar to absorb moisture.

Testing old seed. Before planting seed that is more than one year old, test it. Place ten seeds on a wet cloth and keep them warm for a week. If fewer than six sprout, plant more seed than usual.

Sharing. Share extra seed with neighbours. A variety that grows in many gardens is much less likely to be lost.
