# Solar Water Pump: Maintenance Notes (sample document)

These notes were written for the Kioo demonstration. They describe a small village water pump powered by solar panels. They are an example of the kind of local manual a community might want to keep available without internet.

## Monthly checks

Once a month, clean the solar panels with water and a soft cloth. Do this early in the morning, when the panels are cool. Never pour cold water on hot panels.

Check the cables between the panels and the controller for cracks and for animal damage. Check that the panel frame is firm and that no shade from new branches covers the panels during the middle of the day.

## Pump does not start

If the pump does not start, first check that the panels are clean and that it is daytime with enough light. Then check the controller: a red light means a fault. Switch the controller off, wait one minute, and switch it on again.

If the pump still does not start, check the float switch in the water tank. If the tank is full, the float switch stops the pump on purpose.

## Low water flow

When the flow is weak, look for a blocked inlet screen at the bottom of the well. Pull the pump out only with two people and switch the controller off first. Rinse the screen with clean water.

Check the pipe joints for leaks. A wet patch of ground along the pipe usually shows where a joint has failed.

## Seasonal notes

During the dry season, the well level drops. If the pump runs dry, the controller should stop it. If it does not, switch it off by hand and call the village technician. A pump that runs dry can be damaged within minutes.

Before the rainy season, check the roof of the controller box and make sure the cable entries are sealed with sealant.

## Spare parts

Keep these spare parts in the village store: one float switch, one controller fuse, a roll of sealing tape for pipe threads, and two cable connectors.
